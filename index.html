A baithak has no stage. Everyone sits on the floor, close enough to hear a singer breathe between two lines.

Before concert halls, this is how music travelled in India — a room, a carpet, a lamp, thirty people who came to listen rather than to watch. A singer begins, the tabla answers, someone reads a couplet aloud, and the room replies with wah.

We keep that room alive once a month. No amplification, no ticket queue, no phones out. You arrive at seven, take off your shoes, and stay as long as the music does.

शाम का सिलसिला

A rough order. It changes every time, and it always runs late.

6:45 PM

आग़ाज़ — Doors and chai

Shoes off at the door, kulhad chai in the courtyard. Find a cushion before the lamp is lit.

7:15 PM

सुर-मिलान — Tuning

The tanpura is tuned in front of you. This is part of the evening, not a delay.

7:40 PM

पहला दौर — First set of ghazals

Three or four pieces, unamplified, with the harmonium leading.

8:40 PM

दौर-ए-शायरी — Verse in the round

Anyone may read a couplet aloud. Bring one if you have it; nobody is called on.

9:10 PM

क़िस्सा — The story

One tale, told for about forty minutes, in the old Dastangoi style.

10:00 PM

समा — Sufi kalaam, until it ends

No fixed finish. The evening closes when the room stops asking for one more.

आदाब-ए-महफ़िल

Four things that keep a baithak a baithak.

PHONES AWAY

No recording, no photos during the music. We send you pictures afterwards.

ARRIVE BEFORE 7:15

Once the first note begins we don't open the door again until the break.

SAY WAH

Responding out loud is not an interruption. It's how the singer knows you're there.

FLOOR SEATING

Cushions and bolsters throughout. Tell us if you need a chair and we'll keep one.
